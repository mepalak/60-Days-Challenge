How this was researched

Methodology, scoring logic, and source conflicts ▶

How scores are calculated

Each provider gets a 0–100 sub-score per criterion (Free Tier Value, Ease of Setup, Documentation Quality, Service Breadth), derived from the sourced data points in the table above. Sub-scores are combined using your slider weights (normalized to sum to 100%) to produce the live-updating overall score out of 100. Moving a slider to 0 removes that criterion from the ranking entirely.

Where the hard numbers came from

  • Free tier value: Signup credit amounts, expiry windows, and always-free service counts are taken directly from each provider's official pricing/free-tier pages (AWS Free Tier docs, Google Cloud Free Program page, Azure free account page, DigitalOcean's referral trial terms).
  • Service breadth: "Number of services/products" figures are self-reported marketing counts (AWS: 240+, GCP: 200+, Azure: 65+ always-free tier services cited as a proxy since Microsoft doesn't publish one master count the way AWS/GCP do, DigitalOcean: a much smaller, curated catalog). These are directional, not precise apples-to-apples counts, since providers count "a service" differently.
  • Ease of setup & documentation quality: These two criteria are qualitative by nature. Where sources gave concrete facts (e.g., "requires credit card," "single console tour," "curated one-click apps") those are cited. Where no single authoritative benchmark exists, scores are marked as Estimate and based on the consistent, repeated characterization across the third-party guides cited below (e.g., DigitalOcean and GCP frequently described as more beginner-friendly onboarding flows; AWS and Azure frequently described as having steeper initial learning curves due to console complexity and IAM/permissions setup).
Conflict resolved — DigitalOcean's free trial amount: Sources disagree on whether DigitalOcean's new-signup credit is validated for 60 days uniformly or varies by promo channel (some third-party "promo code" sites cite smaller amounts like $10–$35 via specific referral codes). This app uses the figure DigitalOcean itself and the majority of independent 2026 reviews converge on: $200 in credit, valid 60 days, for new accounts signing up through a standard referral/signup link. Coupon-code sites advertising smaller stacked bonuses were treated as promotional variants, not the baseline offer.
Conflict resolved — AWS free tier structure: Many older articles still describe AWS's pre-July 2025 model (12 months of free EC2/RDS, no credit pool). AWS overhauled this on July 15, 2025. This app uses the current model only: a $100–$200 credit pool valid for up to 6 months, plus 30+ Always Free services with permanent monthly limits, confirmed directly against AWS's own "What's New" announcement and billing documentation.
Conflict resolved — "number of services" claims: AWS's own marketing has cited figures ranging from "200+" to "240+" to "500+ if you count sub-features" across different 2026 sources. We used the most consistently repeated headline figure (240+ fully featured services) rather than the inflated sub-feature count, and applied the same "headline product count" logic to GCP (200+) for comparability.

What's an estimate vs. sourced fact

Every cell in the table above is labeled. Cells without a flag are backed by a specific citable figure or direct quote from an official source. Cells marked Estimate reflect a qualitative judgment synthesized from multiple independent reviewer sources rather than one authoritative number — these exist because "ease of setup" and "doc quality" don't have a single industry-standard benchmark the way "credit amount" does.
